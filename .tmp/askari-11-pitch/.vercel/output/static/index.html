What Havena is

Havena is a community operating system. It is not a chat app and not a public social network.

A community on Havena is a closed group. People join with an invite and a unit number. Only people who belong to that community can see its notices, members, and bookings. Another society cannot see this society’s records.

Three products share the same data:

  • Office dashboard — the building office or board invites members, publishes updates, approves bookings, and manages guests.
  • Resident app and web portal — households read official updates, book facilities, post in the feed, and invite visitors.
  • Guest access — a visitor logs in only for the dates they were given, and only sees what the host allowed.

Havena is already in use at Opus Luxury Apartments, a community in Oman, and a community in Boston.

The problem

Every housing society already does the same five jobs: decide who lives here, decide who may visit, tell residents what is official, share halls and gyms, and collect money for shared costs. Almost none of that work has one system of record. The office rebuilds the picture every week from pieces.

How most offices work today

Membership sits in lists. A spreadsheet of units, a pile of forms, and a WhatsApp message that says “please add my cousin.” When someone moves out, the group is not cleaned. When someone is waiting for approval, the office and the household do not share one status. The guard’s list is not the same as the secretary’s Excel file.

Official news sits in noise. Water shutdowns, security notes, and meeting dates go into the same group as birthday forwards and shop flyers. Residents miss the official line. The office cannot prove who saw it. There is no way to send a reminder only to people who have not confirmed.

Guests sit in a gate book. Someone calls and says “my brother is coming at 7.” The checkpoint has a handwritten name, a screenshot, or nothing. There is no end time, no list of what the visitor may use, and no automatic expiry. Later, the office cannot show who was allowed in that night.

Facilities sit on the phone. The hall or gym is booked by calling the office or messaging a caretaker. Two families can believe they have the same Saturday. There is no slot grid, no capacity rule, and no written approval.

Shared costs sit in cash and memory. Hall fees or a monthly facility charge are paid at the counter or by bank transfer. The slip goes in a drawer. Next month, last month’s unpaid amount is argued from memory. There is no bill, no uploaded proof, no office check, and no receipt the resident can keep.

Local deals sit on flyers. Restaurants post a photo in the group. The resident cannot open a menu inside the same place as the deal.

Email does not look like the society. Codes and circulars, if they exist, come from a personal Gmail or from a platform name the resident does not know. Leadership wants mail to come from the management office.

The problem is not that communities need a new lifestyle app. The problem is that access, communication, facilities, and records are not in one trusted place — and only verified members should be in that place.

The solution

Havena puts the same week in one closed system.

The society is created or requested, then approved. Residents join with an invite code and their unit. If the office needs to review them, they wait in pending until someone accepts them. Then they appear in the directory. They are not “added to a group.” They are members with a clear status: pending, approved, invited, or suspended.

Official updates go out as a notice, an event, or an alert — not as a chat message. Facilities have hours, slots, and capacity. Bookings leave a written yes or no. Guests have a start time, an end time, and a permission list. Shared amenity fees stay with the office cash desk. Havena keeps the record: bill, proof, review, and acknowledgement.

Havena is not a bank. It does not take card payments for hall or gym fees. In Pakistan, the society does not need a card to run the platform. Local shops are not user accounts. They appear in the marketplace after the office approves them.

The path is simple: invite → approve → operate → record. Every step has an owner and a time.

Who uses it

Community admin is the board or the building office. They invite members, publish official updates, approve memberships and bookings, manage guests, keep rules and documents, and moderate the feed and marketplace. They start the day on the dashboard: who is pending, what must be approved, what must be published.

Resident is the household. They join with an invite, complete a profile, and land in a private home for their community only. They read official notices, reply to events, post in the feed, book facilities, message neighbors, follow wellness lessons, use the marketplace, and invite a guest. If they created a facility, they approve who uses it. When a shared bill is due, they pay the office the usual way and upload proof.

Guest is a visitor with an end date. They log in with the invite they were sent. They see only the features the host switched on. When the window ends, or when the office removes them, they are out. They are not a second resident.

Moderator is optional help for day-to-day review. Havena’s own team only approves new communities and new community admins. Businesses are approved listings, not logins.

How a household joins

The administrator signs in on the web with email and a one-time code. They open Members and invite a household: full name, email, phone, and unit. The screen gives an invite code and a QR code. The office sends that code by letter, SMS, or a printed slip. They invite only people who live or work in this community.

The resident opens the resident website or the mobile app. They enter the same email, take the one-time code from that inbox, then enter the invite code or scan the QR. They fill in name and unit. If the office requires approval, they wait. When approved, Home shows this community’s notices, events, and feed. They cannot see another community.

A simple message the office can send:

The society now uses Havena for official notices, events, and guest registration. Sign in with your email, enter the one-time code, then enter this invite code. For help, contact the management office.

Official updates versus the neighbor feed

This is how a water shutdown or a security note stops disappearing in a large group.

A notice is normal office communication. It should say what is happening, when, and what the resident should do. Keep it short. An event has a date, a place, and RSVP. An alert is for urgent cases only. If alerts are used for ordinary meetings, residents stop reading them.

Residents get a push and an in-app notification when the office publishes. House rules can already require a confirmation that the resident has read them. A register of who has read a single notice, and a reminder only to those who have not, is coming.

The feed is where residents post, like, and comment. The office can take a post down. Marketplace listings and deals are not notices.

Today

One WhatsApp group for shutdowns, birthdays, and restaurant flyers. No proof of read. No follow-up list.

On Havena

Notice, event, and alert are office-owned. The feed is resident-owned and moderated. Rule confirmation is live. Notice confirmation and reminder are coming.

Guests and the gate

A visitor is a record, not a WhatsApp name.

A resident or the office creates an invitation: name, phone or email, start time, and end time. They can pick 1 hour, 6 hours, 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, or a custom range. The guest gets an invite code and can use a QR link. They open Havena, verify with a one-time code, and enter the invite. If a resident sent the invite, the host may still need to approve. If the office sent it, access can start inside the window. The office can extend the window, remove access, or check activity. When the window ends, the guest cannot stay signed in.

On every invite, the host turns six areas on or off: feed, notices, events, marketplace, businesses, and wellness. A dinner guest does not need the marketplace. A contractor does not need the community feed.

Connection to a smart lock (such as Yale or Schlage) is coming, after the society confirms the lock model. The guest record and the time window already exist. The lock is the next step, not a claim that Havena opens doors today.

Today

On Havena

Named invite. Time window. Six permission switches. One-time login. Extend or remove. Access ends on its own.

Facilities and bookings

The office registers bookable places: hall, gym, lawn, courts, meeting room. Each one has a name, photo, rules, opening hours, weekend hours, slot length, and capacity. The system will not accept a booking that is over capacity or outside hours. If someone cancels, the slot is free at once.

There are two different approvals. First: should this facility exist? The office creates it, or approves one a resident has submitted. Second: can this person use it tomorrow? The owner of that facility decides. If the hall is office-owned, the office decides. If a resident created a facility the office has allowed, that resident decides. Statuses are pending, confirmed, rejected, and cancelled. Residents can see their own bookings.

When shared billing is on, an unpaid earlier bill can block a new booking until the office clears it. That rule is part of the money section below.

On Havena

A calendar of free slots. A request. A written approval. Capacity enforced.

Daily life for the resident

The mobile app is the daily product. Home shows official notices first, then community activity, deals, wellness, and property. The five tabs are Home, Community, Create, Services, and Profile. The website covers home, notices, feed, messages, wellness, marketplace, and property for people who prefer a browser.

Residents can post in the feed, list an item or service in the marketplace, follow wellness lessons, message neighbors, and invite a guest. Deals are live: an approved business can publish a deal or coupon with a title, vendor, description, image, and dates. Residents can save a deal. A full restaurant menu on a food deal is coming.

2. Notice confirmation and follow-up

Today the office writes “please confirm” in a group. Half the people reply. The office cannot filter the silent half. On Havena, the office already publishes notices and residents get a push. House rules can already require a confirmation. Next, each notice will have a list of who has read it. The office can send a reminder only to those who have not.

Today the office announces a hall or gym charge. People pay at the counter. Proof is a paper slip or a WhatsApp photo. There is no review list and no confirmation the resident can keep. Today “you still owe last month” is an argument at the counter, with two different numbers. Next, an unpaid amount will move to the next bill. The resident will be told the previous unpaid amount and the new total. The office can also block a new facility booking until the earlier bill is cleared.

How shared money works

Havena is not the bank. The office still collects hall and gym fees the way it already does: counter, cash, or bank transfer. Pakistan communities do not need a card to run Havena.

The record will work in four steps:

  1. Publish. The office creates this month’s shared bill for all residents, or for a selected group.
  2. Pay outside. The resident pays at the office or by bank. No card is charged inside Havena.
  3. Proof. The resident uploads the slip. Status: submitted, waiting for review.
  4. Confirm. The office accepts or rejects. The resident sees the confirmation. Unpaid amounts move to the next bill.

The resident sees the amount due, any earlier unpaid amount, the upload, and the office confirmation. The office sees who paid, who uploaded, and who is outstanding — one list to follow.

What we can show live

  1. Add a household. The office invites with name, email, phone, and unit. The resident enters the one-time code and the invite code. The office approves. They appear in the directory.
  2. Publish an update. The office posts a notice or an alert, for example water maintenance from 9am to 1pm. It is not a feed post.
  3. Book a facility. A resident requests a hall or gym slot. The owner approves. The booking is confirmed.
  4. Invite a guest. Create an invite with a four-hour window. Turn off marketplace and feed. The guest logs in with a one-time code. Access ends on its own.
  5. Shared bill. Walk through publish, pay outside, proof, and confirm. Havena does not charge a card for this fee.

A short meeting can cover the first four live, and the shared bill as the close. The useful question is: which of these is the week this office already runs?

In short

The office is already live. Members, guests, official updates, facilities, deals, rules, documents, and moderation are in the product today. Opus Luxury Apartments, a community in Oman, and a community in Boston are already on it.

Access and money follow how communities already work. Verified people. Approved bookings. Amenity payments recorded with proof, not forced onto a card. Guest access that ends on time. Email and locks, where requested, can come from the society — not from a generic platform name.

The next step is a live walk-through. Pick the weekly work this office actually does — invites, notices, guests, facilities, or monthly shared fees — and walk it on the product. Then agree which coming items should land first.
